HOW THIS WORKS

This page doesn't receive anything itself. It tunes a real receiver somewhere in the UK: an OpenWebRX software-defined radio run by a radio amateur, with an antenna on the 2 metre band. What you hear is live.

Picking a channel or turning the knob reloads the receiver on that frequency. The receiver's own panel shows the waterfall and has the volume control.

UK 2m band plan (simplified)

  • 144.000–144.150 CW and moonbounce
  • 144.150–144.400 SSB (144.300 is the centre of activity; FT8 on 144.174)
  • 144.400–144.490 beacons
  • 144.800 APRS packet
  • 144.975–145.1875 repeater inputs (you'll hear little here)
  • 145.200–145.5875 FM simplex, with 145.500 as the calling channel
  • 145.575–145.7875 repeater outputs
  • 145.800–146.000 satellites, including the ISS on 145.800

Repeaters listen 600 kHz below where they transmit. Most analogue ones need a CTCSS tone to open them, but you don't need it to listen.

Receivers are shared: if someone else has one on a different band, you'll see that band until someone switches it back to 2m. Please don't hog a receiver's slots.
